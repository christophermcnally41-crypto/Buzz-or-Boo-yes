But then I'd build out several additional families because this is where the idea becomes a *city operating system for what's next*:

**Beauty & Wellness:** Which salon is surging? Which spa gets hotter? Which fitness concept expands? Will a med-spa open another location?

And then there's a category I think could be particularly powerful:

### 🗣️ "BOSTON SAYS"

These wouldn't necessarily be prediction markets. They're **real-time sentiment questions sitting alongside prediction markets**.

> **Best first-date restaurant right now?**

> **Most overrated Boston restaurant?**

> **Which neighborhood feels like it's about to blow up?**

> **Best new bar?**

> **Which restaurant is impossible to get into but actually worth it?**

Those results don't need to settle for points because they're opinion polls.

But they create **engagement and data between prediction opportunities**.

Then a Boston Says result can *generate a prediction market*.

For example:

**BOSTON SAYS**

> Most likely neighborhood to blow up next?

South End 12%
East Boston **38%**
Dorchester 24%
Allston 17%
Other 9%

↓

AHEAD automatically creates:

> **Will East Boston experience the greatest restaurant-opening growth among these neighborhoods over the next 12 months?**

Now opinion becomes a **testable forecast**.

That loop could be fantastic:

**People talk → AHEAD identifies the signal → market opens → people predict → reality happens → forecasters are scored → rankings change → people talk about the result.**

That's the community engine.

And I think our next working document should become the **AHEAD Market Bible** rather than continuing to generate questions randomly. We put all **120 markets** into categories and define for every one:

**Market # → Display title → Actual question → Market type → Geographic level → Duration → Resolution source → Resolution rule → Update frequency → User scoring → Example app card.**

Then when we add #121, #122, #123, we're building an actual product database rather than accumulating ideas.

That's the point where this starts becoming something you could literally hand to a developer and say: **“Build this.”**
