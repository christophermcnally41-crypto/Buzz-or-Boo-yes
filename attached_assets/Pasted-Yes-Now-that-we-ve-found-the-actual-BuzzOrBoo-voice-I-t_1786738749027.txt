Yes. Now that we've found the **actual BuzzOrBoo voice**, I think there are several changes I'd make to the live product before pushing Instagram traffic there. And importantly, **I would not redesign it.** The visual identity is working. This is mostly about making the experience feel as alive as the new questions.

### 1. Rewrite the existing market headlines first

This is the biggest change.

Some current markets read like the *resolution methodology* instead of the *consumer question*. Keep the rigorous methodology—but move it underneath.

Instead of:

> Which of five Boston restaurant experiences the greatest percentage growth in Google review count during September 2026?

Front-facing:

> **BOSTON'S NEXT IMPOSSIBLE RESERVATION?**

Then underneath:

**HOW WE'LL CALL IT ↗**
*Whichever restaurant has the greatest percentage increase in Google reviews during September.*

That should become a site-wide rule:

**Human question first. Technical resolution second.**

---

### 2. Don't make all 300+ markets equal

This is a big one.

Right now having hundreds of markets sounds impressive, but a new user doesn't need hundreds of choices. **Choice overload could actually make BuzzOrBoo feel duller.**

The homepage should be heavily curated.

I'd want the first thing to be:

**TODAY ON BUZZORBOO**

And maybe **5–8 absolute killers**.

Then:

**🔥 BUZZING NOW**
**📍 BOSTON**
**⚔️ BATTLES**
**🔮 FORECASTS**
**👀 CULTURE**
**STYLE**
**LIFE + MONEY**

And then **MORE QUESTIONS →**

The 300+ markets become depth—not the homepage experience.

---

### 3. Daily 5 needs to become a hero product

I'd make this one of the main reasons to visit every day.

> **THE DAILY 5**
>
> Five things worth having an opinion about today.
>
> **0 / 5 CALLED**

Then after completing them:

> **5/5**
>
> **YOU'VE MADE TODAY'S CALLS.**
>
> Come back tomorrow.

Later:

**+25 BuzzScore** or whatever reward system we settle on.

That gives BuzzOrBoo a beginning and an end each day.

---

### 4. Separate opinion from prediction visually

Right now we have four mechanics, but I want a stranger to understand them without reading instructions.

**BUZZ / BOO**
*Do we like it?*

**BATTLE**
*Which wins?*

**THE CALL**
*What do you think?*

**FORECAST**
*What happens next?*

Forecast cards should feel *slightly* different because those are the ones that resolve and eventually connect to Change.

Maybe a tiny:

**RESOLVES SEP 30**

That instantly communicates:

**Oh—this isn't just a poll. We're going to find out.**

---

### 5. Add “WHY NOW?”

This could be fantastic.

Under:

> **CLOGS ARE CREEPING BACK. WE GOOD WITH THIS?**

Tiny link:

**WHY NOW? ↗**

Tap:

> Searches for clogs have risen X% over the past ___.

Or:

> Three major fall collections featured ___.

Now BuzzOrBoo doesn't feel like we're randomly making shit up.

**We noticed something. That's why we're asking you.**

This becomes especially powerful once the question engine starts using actual trend signals.

---

### 6. Build “THE CROWD” into the payoff

After I vote, don't just show:

**68% BUZZ**

Give me:

> **YOU'RE WITH THE CROWD.**

or

> **👀 YOU'RE IN THE MINORITY.**

And eventually:

> **73% of Boston agrees.**

> **But only 41% of people under 30 do.**

THAT is addictive.

People don't only care what they think.

They desperately want to know **what everybody else thinks.**

---

### 7. Put Change behind Forecasts—not everywhere

I feel even stronger about this after making the 100 questions.

Something like:

> **DANGLY EARRING ON A GUY — HOT OR NOT?**

doesn't need Change.

Just vote.

But:

> **BABY BLUE — NEXT SEASON'S IT COLOR?**

could have:

**🔮 FORECAST**

Then after answering:

> Feeling confident?
>
> 🪙 **PUT YOUR TWO CENTS IN**

**2¢ · 5¢ · 10¢ · 25¢ · 50¢**

That's when Change feels special rather than gimmicky.

---

### 8. We need a killer onboarding moment

Not six tutorial screens.

Something like:

> **WELCOME TO BUZZORBOO**
>
> What's hot.
> What's not.
> What's next.
>
> **Make the call.**

**[START WITH TODAY'S 5]**

Then throw them directly into a question.

Teach by doing.

After their first vote:

> **68% agree with you.**

Done. They understand BuzzOrBoo.

---

### 9. Sign-up should happen AFTER they get the dopamine

This is important.

I don't want:

Instagram → BuzzOrBoo → **CREATE AN ACCOUNT**

before they've done anything.

Let them make a few calls.

Then:

> **YOU'VE MADE 3 CALLS.**
>
> Save your picks and start building your BuzzScore.
>
> **CREATE YOUR PROFILE**

Now there's a reason.

---

### 10. Results need to become content

Once something resolves:

> **YOU CALLED IT.**

Or:

> **BOSTON CALLED IT.**

Or:

> **THE CROWD BLEW IT.**

😂

Those become beautiful shareable cards.

Imagine Instagram:

**THE CROWD BLEW IT.**

*Only 31% predicted ___.*

**BUZZORBOO**

That's content generated by the product itself.

---

### 11. Give people a reason to share their identity

Eventually profile cards could say:

> **CHRISTOPHER**
>
> STYLE — 82%
> BOSTON — 76%
> CULTURE — 71%
>
> **TOP 9% OF BOSTON CALLERS**

**SHARE**

Now BuzzOrBoo starts escaping BuzzOrBoo.

People aren't sharing an ad for us.

They're sharing **something about themselves.**

---

### 12. “Wake-Up Call” should NOT be another market category

This is one place I'd restrain us.

I love **WAKE-UP CALL**, but I think it should be **editorial**, not another button cluttering the navigation.

Occasionally BuzzOrBoo says:

> 🚨 **WAKE-UP CALL**
>
> There's something you should know about.

Could be a documentary, brand movement, Boston opening, emerging trend, weird statistic, etc.

Then underneath it can lead into an actual question.

That makes BuzzOrBoo feel like it has **editors with taste**, not just an algorithm generating polls.

---

### 13. Add “Where Are They Now?” similarly

I wouldn't make that a core market type either.

It's a **content franchise**.

> **WHERE ARE THEY NOW?**
>
> MISCHA BARTON

Then perhaps:

**Still Buzz?**

It's Instagram/editorial material that can lead into BuzzOrBoo interaction.

---

### 14. Situational Boston needs its own personality

This might become one of our strongest acquisition channels.

Not:

> Best Boston restaurants

That's boring and Google owns it.

BuzzOrBoo:

> **WHERE DO YOU BRING YOUR MOTHER?**

> **WHERE DO YOU TAKE SOMEONE YOU'RE TRYING TO IMPRESS?**

> **WHERE DO YOU GO WHEN SOMEONE ELSE IS PAYING?**

> **BEST FIRST DATE WHEN YOU'RE NOT SURE IT'S A DATE?**

Then **the crowd ranks the answers**.

That's genuinely useful.

Eventually somebody Googles:

**best first date Boston**

and BuzzOrBoo has an entire crowd-ranked answer.

That could become a real SEO engine too.

---

### 15. Finally: reduce explanation everywhere

This may be the biggest *design* change without changing the design.

BuzzOrBoo should feel **fast**.

Question.

Tap.

Result.

Next.

The deeper methodology is available for people who want it:

**WHY NOW?**
**HOW THIS RESOLVES**
**SOURCE**
**DISCUSSION**

But don't make everyone read it.

The sophisticated machinery should be almost invisible.

---

If I were prioritizing Replit work **right now**, I would *not* dump all 100 new questions into it yet.

I'd first make **20 absolutely killer questions**—including Revere, Coach, Trader Joe's/Whole Foods, $65K Boston, clogs, dangling earring, first-date Boston, etc.—and use those to rebuild the **first-screen/homepage experience** around:

> **TODAY ON BUZZORBOO**
> **THE DAILY 5**
> **BUZZING NOW**
> **BOSTON**
> **MORE QUESTIONS**

Because when someone clicks from that gorgeous Instagram grid, I want their first reaction to the website to be:

**“Ohhhhh. I get it.”**

And their second reaction:

**“Wait—I need to answer that.”**

That's the change I'd make before worrying about anything technical.
