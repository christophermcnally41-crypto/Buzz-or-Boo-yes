### The important thing: these shouldn't all behave identically

This first 71 gives the app several **market formats**, which makes the product much more interesting.

Some are binary:

> **Will chrome surpass brass?**
> YES 62% / NO 38%

Some are multi-choice:

> **What's the next major fashion color?**
> Burgundy 31% / Chocolate 27% / Blue 21%...

Some concern a threshold:

> **Will Boston get more than 12" of snow this month?**

Some concern a specific asset/property:

> **Will this condo sell above $1.4M?**

And some are head-to-head:

> **Dark wood vs. pale oak**

That variety is important because we're discovering which **kind of prediction people become obsessed with making.**

There is also something I want built into these from the beginning: every market gets a hidden administrative record containing **Market ID, exact question, category, open date, close date, resolution date, resolution source, resolution formula, acceptable data, cancellation criteria, and result**.

That's ultimately what separates this from a fun polling app. The question has to be **provably right or wrong**.

And I would absolutely make **#22–24—the individual-property markets—a signature feature**. Imagine opening AHEAD Boston, seeing a gorgeous Back Bay listing and discovering that 4,800 Boston users collectively think there's a **76% chance it sells above asking**. That's the kind of feature people would screenshot and share.

We now essentially have the seed content for **Version 1 of the product**. Next I'd turn these 71 into the actual **market database structure**, including their resolution sources, durations, point payouts, and exactly what the cards say in the app, so a developer could begin building from it.
