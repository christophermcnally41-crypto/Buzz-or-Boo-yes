That's significant because **the matching .com + Instagram handle** gives you a clean starting point for the brand.

And now that we've developed the product further, I like **BuzzOrBoo** even more because it works on several levels:

**Buzz** = something is gaining momentum.
**Boo** = something isn't.
**Buzz** = what people are talking about.
**Buzz** = local cultural intelligence.
**Buzz or Boo?** = the actual action users take.

I'd formalize the working brand as:

# BUZZORBOO

### **Call what's next.**

And inside the product, we can develop language that's uniquely ours:

**What's Buzzing** — discovery feed
**Boston Buzz** — local feed
**Buzz Battle** — head-to-head markets
**Buzz Score** — probability/trend measurement
**Buzzed It** — user's positive prediction
**Boo'd It** — user's negative prediction
**Early Buzz** — emerging trends
**Biggest Movers** — rapidly changing predictions
**My Calls** — user's active forecasts
**Called It** — resolved predictions they nailed
**BuzzRank** — forecaster ranking

That begins creating an actual **brand vocabulary**, which is much harder for competitors to copy than simply having a prediction interface.

There is one thing I'd do **right now before publicly announcing the name or spending meaningful money on branding**: run a proper trademark clearance search for **BUZZORBOO / BUZZ OR BOO / similar marks**, especially around software, social networking, market research, entertainment, forecasting, financial services, and potentially gaming. Owning the domain and Instagram handle is excellent, but it doesn't automatically establish trademark clearance.

After that, I think our two workstreams should run together:

**PRODUCT:** Build the **BuzzOrBoo Market Bible**, starting with the 120 markets we've created and expanding the local categories.

**BRAND/UX:** Build the first BuzzOrBoo interface—logo direction, typography, navigation, prediction cards, Boston Buzz, Home, Style, profiles and BuzzRank.

Then we can create an actual clickable MVP specification rather than just an idea.

And I would preserve one principle from everything we've figured out today:

> **BuzzOrBoo isn't an app for betting on what happens.**
>
> **It's where people call what's next.**

That distinction should guide almost every product and branding decision from here.
